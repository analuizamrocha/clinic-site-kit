## What the previous draft got wrong

Correct these if you see them copied into a project doc:

1. **Before/after is not banned.** Art. 14 allows patient images and stock
   images for education, including outcome demonstrations, under the
   conditions below. The revoked 1.974/2011 banned showing the patient to
   advertise a result. A project may still choose not to use before/after
   at all. That is an editorial choice, stricter than the legal floor.
2. **"Maximum 2 testimonials per semester" is a Codame definition of
   repetition, not a content quota in the resolution.** The manual says a
   reshare of patient praise is "reiterada" when it happens more than twice
   in a semester. Repeated praise is investigated even when the doctor does
   not reshare it (art. 8, §4).
3. **"At least four different patients" is manual practice, not art. 14.**
   The article requires a set that includes indications, satisfactory
   outcomes, unsatisfactory outcomes and complications. The practical
   chapter turns that into four stages and at least four patients.

## Identification

**Art. 4 — every advertising piece**

- Name.
- CRM number(s) for the state(s) where the doctor practices, next to the
  word MÉDICO.
- Specialty and/or area of practice, when registered at the CRM, followed
  by the RQE number.

**Art. 6 — sites, blogs and social profiles**

The art. 4 block goes on the main page of the profile (person or clinic),
or the equivalent. Stories and other temporary posts follow the same
resolution (art. 6, §1). A profile that mixes private life and medical
topics still has to carry the identification (art. 6, §3). A purely private
profile that never discusses medicine does not (Manual, §7.1).

A blog footer with name, specialty, CRM and RQE is stricter than art. 6 and
is the right default: a post is often read without the homepage. Do not
treat a missing footer as an automatic violation of art. 6 if the site
header or about page already carries the block. Do treat a site with no
identification anywhere as a violation.

**Titles (art. 13, §1)**

- Specialist means RQE. Announce at most two specialties, plus the related
  areas of practice.
- A lato or stricto sensu postgraduate title registered at the CRM is
  announced as MÉDICO(A) with that title, followed by NÃO ESPECIALISTA in
  capitals. It does not get a number.
- A non-specialist must not advertise treating a specific organ, system or
  disease in a way that reads as a specialty (art. 11, I).

## Prohibited — art. 11

- Guarantee, promise or suggest a good treatment result (XII).
- "Médico do ano", "melhor médico", "destaque da especialidade" and similar
  sponsored ranking lists (XIII).
- Sensationalism (art. 11, §2): exalting the doctor's own action; publicising
  a method the CFM has not recognised; manipulating statistics; showing a
  technique or a procedure that belongs in a medical setting; causing fear,
  insecurity or panic, including about known facts; seductive visuals that
  imply a guaranteed result.
- Self-promotion (art. 11, §3): assigning privileged qualities to oneself,
  to the service, or to a technique. Being the only one who performs a
  technique does not allow a claim of privileged capacity (IX).
- Unfair competition (art. 11, §4): miracle or extraordinary discoveries
  behind extra clicks, personal data or payment; disrespectful attacks on
  other doctors, specialties or techniques; advertising free care in a
  private office; a prevention campaign with no named sponsor.
- Untrue content (art. 11, §5): revolutionary or miraculous practices, or
  procedures the CFM has not approved for medical use.
- Equipment or medicine without Anvisa registration (III). Privileged
  capacity assigned to a device (II). Commercial brands of implants and
  supplies (art. 9, XVIII, c).
- Live images of a consultation or a procedure, even with consent.
  Exceptions: childbirth filmed by an outside crew when the patient wants
  it and the doctor agrees (art. 14, II, d); real-time images inside a
  scientific event restricted to doctors and medical students, with prior
  authorisation (art. 13, IV).
- A lay course, post or chatbot that delivers a diagnosis, a judgment on
  which procedure to have, or a prognosis. Teaching an act that is exclusive
  to physicians is forbidden (art. 9, XII; art. 11, XI, except telemedicine
  under its own resolution).

## Own site versus an interview

On the doctor's own site, blog or social profile, publicity may aim to
form, keep or grow a patient base, and may include how to book, hours,
consultation prices, payment methods and the fact that a private procedure
fee can be agreed before the visit (art. 8, §2; art. 9, IV–VIII; art. 13,
III). Discounts are allowed when they are not tied to bundled sales or
prizes (art. 9, VIII).

In an interview, or in an article published in mass media, the doctor
speaks as a representative of medicine: no client-seeking, no claim of an
exclusive method, no address or phone, and conflicts of interest must be
declared (art. 10). Only the art. 4 identification may appear.

A reshare of a patient's post becomes the doctor's post (art. 8, §3).

## Patient images and testimonials

**Educational images (art. 14).** Allowed when they explain a disease or a
procedure inside the registered specialty: signs that should lead someone
to seek care, and the technical options. Any outcome demonstration needs:

- Educational text with indications, factors that change the result, and
  complications described in the scientific literature (II, a).
- Before/after shown as a set: indications, satisfactory outcomes,
  unsatisfactory outcomes and complications. No teaching of a technique
  that belongs in a medical setting (II, b).
- Different body types, ages, and immediate, intermediate and late
  results, when that applies (II, c).
- No image of a procedure that identifies the patient (II, e).
- No editing, manipulation or improvement (II, f). The practical chapter
  allows crop, light, sharpness and bars that protect identity or modesty,
  provided the result is not distorted.
- Stock images credited under copyright (II, h).
- The doctor's own images: written authorisation, modesty and privacy, and
  anonymity even when the patient authorised disclosure (II, i).

**Manual, aplicação prática, cap. XII — operational, not the article text.**
A before/after piece has four stages: the condition, at least four different
patients before, at least four after, and unsatisfactory results or
complications (illustration, photo or text is enough for the last stage).
In a compiled video, each frame stays at least two seconds. The practical
chapter also forbids images of female breasts, the gluteal region and the
intimate region, forbids paying or discounting care in exchange for the
image, and says consent can be withdrawn. Treat that chapter as Codame
practice. If a project needs those images, re-read the chapter before
publishing and record the date.

**Testimonials (art. 14, II, g, plus the manual).** A reshared selfie or
testimonial stays sober: no adjective of superiority, no implied promise of
a result. The manual defines a repeated reshare as more than twice per
semester, and treats that repetition as sensationalism and unfair
competition. Systematic praise on the patient's own profile, even when the
doctor never reshares it, is something Codame investigates (art. 8, §4).

## Tone

Sober, impersonal where a bulletin is involved (art. 12), and true.
Educational copy on the doctor's own site can still describe the practice,
prices and how to book. It cannot frighten, guarantee, rank the doctor
above colleagues, or present a method as exclusive.

Prefer "pode", "em casos selecionados", "depende da avaliação individual".

## Artificial intelligence — Resolução 2.454/2026

In force since 26/08/2026. It adds duties. It does not relax 2.336.

- The doctor stays fully responsible for acts done with AI (art. 7).
- AI used to advertise or promote must still meet the advertising rules
  (art. 7, §3).
- Do not let a system tell a patient a diagnosis, a prognosis or a
  treatment decision without a physician in between (art. 5, §2). A site
  chatbot may give hours and booking information. It may not say what the
  person has.
- Tell the patient, in plain language, when AI is a relevant support to
  that person's care, diagnosis or treatment (art. 5, §1). This pass did
  not find a rule that an educational blog post must be labelled as
  machine-written. Do not invent one. The physician still answers for the
  text.

## Checklist before a blog post ships

1. The site's main identification block matches art. 4, and the post footer
   repeats name, specialty, CRM and RQE.
2. No guaranteed, promised or suggested result.
3. No "melhor", "exclusivo", "único", "milagroso", "revolucionário", and no
   ranking title.
4. No fear hook, including about a real disease.
5. No identifiable patient, record, or case detail. No before/after unless
   the art. 14 set is actually present and the manual's four-stage sequence
   was re-read for this piece.
6. Any reshared patient praise is sober, and this semester's reshares are
   still within the manual's "not more than twice" line.
7. Claims a lay reader could act on are backed by mainstream literature, and
   the post does not diagnose the reader or choose their procedure.
8. Equipment mentions stay inside the Anvisa portfolio and do not claim a
   privileged device.
9. If the post will also run as a press article or interview, strip address,
   phone and any client-seeking close, and declare conflicts (art. 10).
10. Anything an AI drafted was read by a person against this list. A bot is
    not the one delivering the clinical conclusion.

## Enforcement path the resolution actually describes

Codame can summon the doctor or the technical director, tell them to take
the piece down, record that in minutes, and send a possible ethics breach
to the regional council's corregedoria for a sindicância (art. 16). It also
monitors the internet. This pass did not re-read the Código de Ética Médica
penalty scale. Do not map a wording slip to "license revocation" without
that text.

When a rule is unclear, the doctor may ask the regional Codame (art. 13, V).
Say so instead of guessing.
